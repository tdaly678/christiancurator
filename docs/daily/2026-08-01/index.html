Daily Pulse

August 1, 2026

Today the church is asking how you actually know whether faith is real — and the answers keep pointing to the same uncomfortable place: not feelings, not certainty, but love. The Aquila Report runs the classic test: 1 John's claim that love for others is the one proof that cannot be faked. That lands differently alongside Carey Nieuwhof's piece on what AI is doing to discipleship. His argument is blunt: we have trained ourselves to expect instant answers, and God refuses to give them. Patient waiting is the form love takes. AI makes that harder. Ligonier is making a related point about Scripture — that the Bible's depths only open to people who slow down enough to let them. Three different pieces, one diagnosis: the speed of modern life is producing a faith too shallow to prove itself.

9Marks runs a piece on Melchizedek that most readers will skim past — but shouldn't. The argument is that priesthood was never a solo vocation. It was always corporate, always tied to belonging. That cuts directly against the instinct Relevant Magazine flags: church invitations fail because people don't want an event, they want to be known. Mere Orthodoxy takes a longer view — Luke writing Acts into Greek tragic form, positioning Paul as the figure the myths were reaching for but couldn't produce. A savior the community didn't know it needed. Earlier this week we were watching this same tension between desire and formation — what we actually want versus what we were made for. It hasn't resolved. It's just showing up now in questions about belonging, about who holds the priest's office, about whether the church can be the thing it keeps promising to be.
