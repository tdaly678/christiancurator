Daily Pulse

April 13, 2026

Today the church is asking whether it actually knows what it's offering — whether pastors, theologians, and ordinary believers have a clear enough grip on the Gospel to pass it on. The Gospel Coalition's podcast with everyday pastors puts it bluntly: if pastors can't define the Gospel, they'll lose it. Not dramatically. Quietly. Carl Trueman's work, reviewed at The Aquila Report, runs the same logic in the other direction — strip God from the center of human identity, and humans become disposable. First Things also ran Trueman's diagnosis of modernity as something the church has to understand before it can treat. These three pieces are saying the same thing in different registers: vagueness isn't neutral. It costs people.

Desiring God and Mere Orthodoxy land on the same problem from opposite ends. Desiring God goes hard on sin — pleasure chosen over holiness will destroy you, full stop. Mere Orthodoxy is gentler but no less serious: mindfulness doesn't save, because it has no Giver to return to. Christianity Today's piece on infertility sits in the tension between those two. It pushes back on the evangelical instinct to give suffering people easy answers — 'just pray' lands as dismissal when the ache is that deep. On March 30 we were watching the same tension play out around desire and formation. It hasn't resolved. First Things also ran a quiet, almost confessional piece about faithful theological work that never finds an audience — devotion without reward. A climber in another First Things essay confronts mortality on a mountain and can't look away. Both pieces ask the same question beneath the surface: is faithfulness worth it when nothing guarantees the outcome? The church this week seems to believe the answer is yes, but it's not pretending the question is easy.
