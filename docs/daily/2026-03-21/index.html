Daily Pulse

March 21, 2026

A recurring question across today's content is whether the church's engagement with culture is being shaped more by urgency than by faithfulness. Finding Faithfulness Beyond the Pressure to Rush argues that the speed of social media controversy is conditioning Christians to react rather than discern, collapsing the space needed for careful theological judgment. The Gospel Coalition's piece on False Gospels Extend Beyond the Digital World moves in a related direction, contending that distorted gospels aren't only a platform problem — they take root in embodied communities too. Meanwhile, American Reformer's Faith and Division: Understanding Our Fragmented Times locates the church's fractures within a broader cultural dissolution of shared authority and common life. These pieces don't fully agree on diagnosis: one treats the problem as primarily formational and spiritual, another as institutional and civilizational. The disagreement matters because the proposed remedies — personal discipleship, confessional retrieval, or structural resistance — point in different directions.

A quieter thread running through today's content concerns who counts as a full participant in the church's life and witness. Mere Orthodoxy's World Down Syndrome Day reflection makes a theological case that relationality with God is not conditioned on cognitive capacity, pushing back implicitly against anthropologies that tie spiritual standing to rational agency. Christianity Today's piece on children's views of God covers similar ground from a different angle, examining what it means to take seriously the theological instincts of those the church often treats as recipients rather than participants. A federal court's order allowing clergy into a Minneapolis ICE detention facility — covered by World Magazine — raises the same structural question in a legal register: whether pastoral access is a right that extends to the margins of civic life.
