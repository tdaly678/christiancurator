Daily Pulse

March 23, 2026

The church is asking today whether it has anything real to offer the loneliest generation in American history — and the answer coming back is complicated. The Gospel Coalition frames Gen Z's loneliness as a crisis the church is uniquely equipped to meet. First Things is more cautious, tracing Gen Z's spiritual tendencies with enough nuance to suggest the church's answer only lands if it's honest about what young people are actually bringing through the door. Scot McKnight is watching the data on who counts as religious now and the picture is messier than either outlet admits. That question hasn't gone away — on March 21 several writers were already pressing whether the church knows who it's speaking to before it opens its mouth.

Mere Orthodoxy ran a World Down Syndrome Day piece asking whether the church receives people with intellectual disabilities as full theological participants — not as recipients of ministry, but as those who know God. A hard question, and a clarifying one. Christianity Today meanwhile is telling readers that panic is not a spiritual gift — faithfulness is slow, and urgency is often just anxiety in a clerical collar. Dozens were killed in a Sudan hospital bombing during Eid, a detail the feed mostly passed over. Quebec's religious symbol ban is moving through Canadian courts, and World Magazine's legal podcast is tracking where those lines hold. The thread running under all of it: the church keeps debating what it owes the world while the world keeps showing up with needs that don't wait for the debate to finish.
