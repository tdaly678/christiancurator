March 22, 2026

The question underneath most of today's feed is the same one we keep circling: what does faithfulness actually look like when the world keeps accelerating? Christianity Today says urgency is not faithfulness — that the pressure to respond fast to every scandal and controversy is itself a spiritual formation problem. The Gospel Coalition is less patient about pace and more alarmed about content: false gospels aren't just spreading through screens anymore, they're moving through neighborhoods, small groups, family dinners. One outlet says slow down. The other says the poison is already in the room. Both are responding to the same fracture — they just disagree on where the bleeding is worst.

Joy Clarkson's piece cuts through both arguments. Love, she writes, is the most practical foundation for Christian living — not a sentiment but a structure. That lands differently against a federal judge ordering a Minneapolis ICE facility to allow clergy access to detainees. Pastoral presence as a legal fight. The church showing up, literally, for people the state has made hard to reach. Mere Orthodoxy's World Down Syndrome Day reflection asks the same thing from a different angle — who counts as a full participant in the life of the community? And Christianity Today's reporting on Christians surviving the Pakistan-Afghanistan conflict puts all of it in relief. Faithfulness, for some people, is not a social media posture. It is a daily survival question. The feed today keeps returning to who the church is actually for — and whether love is a feeling we talk about or a place we show up.
