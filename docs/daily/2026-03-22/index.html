March 22, 2026

Today the feed is full of arguments about what Christians are supposed to tolerate — in worship, in art, in theology, in law. The Gospel Coalition and American Reformer are essentially pulling in opposite directions. TGC tells the reader who dislikes church music to stay anyway, submit, grow. American Reformer says the whole framework of theological triage — ranking doctrines so we know what to fight over — is itself the problem. It flattens the faith into a checklist. These aren't the same argument. Christianity Today's piece on urgency runs underneath both: the impulse to draw lines fast, to act before discerning, is not faithfulness. That question hasn't gone away — on March 21 several writers were circling the same tension between speed and fidelity. Bethel McGrew at Further Up adds Flannery O'Connor to the mix. Her point is that Christians who can't stomach O'Connor's grotesque, violent grace may be protecting a version of faith that's more comfortable than true. Discomfort, it turns out, is the thread running through all of it.

The World Down Syndrome Day reflection at Mere Orthodoxy is the quietest piece in the feed and maybe the sharpest. It asks whether the church has actually internalized what it confesses about image-bearers — or whether inclusion is still a program rather than a posture. The Wall Street Journal's report on Uganda's anti-gay law is harder to sit with. Police and criminals are both using it to extort and abuse. Whatever you believe about the law's origins, that's a church-and-state failure with real bodies attached. A Louisiana deacon, meanwhile, is waiting on an appeal after being excommunicated — reportedly for speaking publicly about a priest who abused his son. The Guardian has the details. Institutional self-protection wearing the vestments of discipline. Desiring God runs a piece on finding beauty in suffering this week, and it's not wrong, but next to Uganda and Louisiana it lands differently than it probably intended.
