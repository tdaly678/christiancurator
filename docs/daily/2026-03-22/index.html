Daily Pulse

March 22, 2026

Today's feed keeps returning to one question: what holds the church together when everything is pulling it apart? American Reformer is bluntest about it — we are in an age of fracture, and the church is not immune. The Gospel Coalition agrees the pressure is real, tracing how false gospels that used to live online have migrated into actual congregations, actual relationships, actual Sunday mornings. And Christianity Today says the speed is the problem — urgency masquerading as faithfulness, crisis posture becoming the only posture Christians know. That question hasn't gone away — on March 21 several writers were circling this same tension between discernment and pace, between holding fast and being swept along.

Holding all of this is harder than it sounds. Joy Clarkson wrote this week that love — not strategy, not urgency, not doctrinal precision — is the most practical thing a person can choose. It reads almost naively against the fracture pieces. But Clarkson isn't being naive. She's naming something the fracture conversation tends to skip: the church coheres through love or it doesn't cohere. Mere Orthodoxy runs a grief piece that makes the same move from a different direction — grief strips the performance away and leaves something truer. Meanwhile a Canadian Supreme Court case is forcing the question of whether religious expression survives in secular public life at all, BBC News reporting that women's rights advocates are split on a secularism law that some see as neutral and others see as exclusion with better branding. The church can argue about its own fractures. The world is not waiting.
