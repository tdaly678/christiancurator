Daily Pulse

March 30, 2026

Today the church is asking a deceptively simple question: what is the Gospel actually for? The Gospel Coalition ran a conversation with Garrett Kell about preaching evangelistically — not just informatively, but with intent toward conversion. TGC also posted separately on what Christians should know about conversion itself. Lifeway Research added a piece on sin as the necessary foundation for any of this to make sense. Three pieces, same spine: the Gospel has a specific shape, and losing that shape has consequences. Christianity Today reviewed Arthur Brooks on life's purpose, and his answer drifts toward self-actualization — meaningful, perhaps, but not quite the same thing as repentance and new birth. That gap matters. Earlier this week we were watching this same tension between transformation as real event versus transformation as gradual self-improvement. It hasn't resolved.

First Things published two pieces worth holding together. One is a meditation on the difference between worldly and sacred affections — the interior question of what the heart actually loves. The other is an interview with Frank DeVito about JD Vance's faith, which is the exterior version of the same question: what does professed belief actually produce? Neither piece answers the other, but the friction is productive. The Guardian reported that churches across the country are converting unused property into affordable housing — a concrete answer to the question of what the church is for. Christianity Today covered the Supreme Court ruling that schools cannot conceal students' gender transitions from parents, a decision that lands differently depending on whether you think the family or the institution is the primary site of formation. CT also reported on thousands of Kenyan men deceived into fighting in Ukraine — a story about human dignity and exploitation that the church has no obvious political script for, only a theological one.
