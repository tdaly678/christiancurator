Daily Pulse

November 23, 2026

Evangelical writers are asking this week whether the church forms people who are actually prepared to live as Christians in ordinary life, or whether it settles for something thinner. Desiring God's Marshall Segal puts the pressure on preaching: shorter attention spans tempt preachers toward novelty, but gimmicks cannot do what the Word does. The Gospel Coalition's Matt Rusten takes the same question into the pew, arguing that the New Testament gives preachers two distinct frameworks for speaking to workers, and that most sermons use neither. Daniel Darling at Lifeway Research adds a third angle: discipleship that stops at Sunday cannot produce the boldness the moment requires.

Cornelis Venema at Ligonier argues that the formation question has a doctrinal floor: without a recovered theology of assurance, Christians cannot live with the confidence genuine witness demands. That concern about inner conviction connects to the praxis end of the feed. Amanda Held Opelt at Christianity Today argues that feasting itself is a formative practice, one the church has ceded to anxious consumer culture. Matt Bennett and Ryan Robertson at Crossway press missionaries specifically: sin left unaddressed hollows out even the most earnest calling. The thread across all of it is the same: formation is not ambient. It requires doctrine, practice, and honest self-examination working together, and the writers here are mostly dissatisfied with how often the church treats one of the three as sufficient.
