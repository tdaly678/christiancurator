Daily Pulse

June 25, 2026

The church is asking this week who belongs — at the Table, in the family, in the body politic — and what it costs to say so clearly. Christianity Today ran a piece from a father of a daughter with Down syndrome that refuses to argue on the world's terms. Quality of life isn't the point, he says. Full stop. That sits next to World Magazine's conversation about how history's worst ideas — eugenics, dehumanization — don't return through argument. They return through forgetting. Specifically through forgetting bodies. The Gospel Coalition is also asking a belonging question, though from the inside: who comes to the Table, and on whose authority? Four views, genuine disagreement, and no clean resolution. That's not a weakness of the piece. It's the point.

Christianity Today is warning pastors directly: with the Fourth of July approaching, the question isn't how to do a patriotic service well — it's whether the categories are compatible at all. Gospel or flag. Not both. That tension between faithfulness and cultural pressure has been running through this feed for weeks — on March 26 several writers were pressing on what genuine faith looks like when it's under that kind of weight. 9Marks adds something grimmer to the picture: pastoral ministry doesn't usually end in scandal. It ends in slow erosion — a thousand small cuts that nobody names until it's too late. Ligonier's case for elders and TGC's conversation about passing faith to the next generation are both responding, in different registers, to the same institutional fragility. The church has structures for a reason. The question is whether anyone still believes that.
