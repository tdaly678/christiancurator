Daily Pulse

April 11, 2026

Today the church is asking what it actually costs to follow Jesus — and whether most American Christianity has quietly opted out of finding out. The Aquila Report runs a piece this week about encountering the biblical Jesus — the one who demands, not just comforts — and losing the manageable version in the process. Jenn Johnson is making a similar argument from the worship side: Relevant Magazine covers her warning that worship without obedience is performance, full stop. And Lifeway Research is asking whether churches even ask anything hard of their congregations anymore — three strategies for cultivating sacrifice, because apparently congregations willing to give something up don't just appear. That question hasn't gone away — earlier this week the conversation was about desire and formation, about whether our minds are even capable of wanting the right things. The cost question is the next one downstream.

Christianity Today is running a dispatch on Iran's underground Christians — people paying with their freedom and sometimes their lives for a faith that costs something by definition. That story sits next to another CT piece arguing that throwing around words like "profound evil" in geopolitical threats cheapens both the language and the theology of just war. Real cost requires real precision. Mere Orthodoxy is more blunt: evangelicals traded one false politics for another and forfeited their moral credibility in the process. Ligonier is asking whether the atonement itself was coercive — did God need to kill his Son, or was that something else entirely. And a Mississippi farmer has quietly welcomed 150 Ukrainian refugee families onto his land, which Christianity Today covers without much editorializing, because it doesn't need any. Costly discipleship apparently still looks like what it always did.
