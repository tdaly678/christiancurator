Daily Pulse

April 11, 2026

Today the church is asking what it costs to mean what you say — in worship, in doctrine, in politics, and under fire. Iran is the sharpest edge of that question right now. Christianity Today's report on Iran's underground church is not an inspiring sidebar. It's a indictment of comfortable faith. These believers are losing jobs, families, and freedom for confessing Christ. Jenn Johnson is working a related nerve from a very different angle — Relevant Magazine ran her warning that American worship has drifted into performance, feeling untethered from actual obedience. Doug Moo puts the same pressure in Greek: faith without works, he argues at Daily Dose of Greek, won't survive judgment. Three different sources, same diagnosis — the church keeps splitting what belongs together.

Mere Orthodoxy is running two pieces that pull in opposite directions, and both matter. One traces how evangelicals swapped libertarian politics for authoritarian ones — a false gospel traded for another false gospel, the price paid in credibility and coherence. The other is quieter: a meditation on holy indolence, on slowing down enough to see what's real. That tension hasn't gone away — earlier this week we were watching it surface in the conversation about pace and modern life. Christianity Today also published a just-war piece this week arguing that threatening 'profound evil' without moral grammar trivializes evil itself — words untethered from doctrine become weapons. And then there's a Mississippi farmer who took in Ukrainian refugees during harvest season. No argument. No platform. Just sacrifice at the worst possible time. That's the thing the church keeps talking around and occasionally just doing.
