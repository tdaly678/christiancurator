Daily Pulse

July 4, 2026

It's the Fourth of July, and the church is asking whether America and Christianity actually belong together — and what happens when that question gets weaponized. Mere Orthodoxy argues the Declaration's logic requires something like belief — that ordered liberty without transcendent grounding eventually collapses into itself. Christianity Today lands nearby, reading Augustine into the American experiment: a restless nation that won't find rest outside God. Then there's Eric Metaxas on World Magazine, arguing Christian faith — not Enlightenment rationalism — built the country. O. Alan Noble pushes back hard. His newsletter this week warns that crisis narratives about America's collapse are often manufactured — tools for mobilizing fear more than honest diagnosis. Noble and Metaxas are not having the same conversation, even when they use the same words.

Underneath the patriotic noise, though, a quieter set of questions is running. Desiring God frames pornography not as moral failure but as spiritual warfare with civilizational stakes — closer to the culture-war register than a pastoral one. First Things takes the opposite angle entirely, sitting with Arvo Pärt and asking whether transcendence in art is genuine encounter or just aesthetic retreat. Christianity Today runs a piece on what happens to faith when the people who shaped it fall — a pastoral question that keeps surfacing this season. Earlier this week we were watching the same tension between transformation and disillusionment work through discussions of sanctification and identity. It hasn't resolved. Sean McDowell's interview with J. Budziszewski — a nihilist philosopher who couldn't outrun God — is the most quietly compelling item of the day. Not because it's triumphalist. Because it's honest about how long resistance actually takes.
