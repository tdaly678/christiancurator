Daily Pulse

July 31, 2026

Today the church is asking who — and what — actually does the work of formation. Mere Orthodoxy and The Gospel Coalition are both watching AI move into pastoral space, and neither is comfortable with it. Mere Orthodoxy's concern is loneliness — that people are letting AI fill relational voids that require embodied presence. TGC says the problem is authority: a chatbot that sounds wise is still not a pastor, and sounding wise is not the same thing as being accountable to a congregation. The distinction matters. Formation requires someone who stays. The Aquila Report ran a piece on ministry and endurance this week — the argument is that Paul's model isn't excellence, it's persistence. Showing up when it costs something. That's a different category than any algorithm can occupy. Christianity Today adds another pressure point: a generation of evangelical students who can't place Billy Graham. If the tradition isn't being handed down, the question of who does the handing becomes urgent.

Tim Challies is asking whether any heart is actually beyond reach — which is a prayer question before it's a theology question. The answer shapes how the church intercedes, and for whom. That sits alongside Relevant Magazine's report on a woman whose job offer abroad became a human trafficking nightmare — technology enabling exploitation at scale, the church largely absent from the frame. American Reformer has a quieter counter-signal: Sweden's secular consensus is fracturing, and religious identity is filling the gap. Across the Atlantic, something is reaching people the institutions couldn't. Back home, Christianity Today is tracking Christian nationalism moving through Presbyterian denominational meetings — not as fringe noise but as structured debate. That's a different kind of formation question. The earlier tension this week around desire and discernment hasn't resolved — it's just wearing new clothes. What shapes us, who we trust to do it, and whether we can still tell the difference.
