Daily Pulse

July 8, 2026

Today the church is asking a question it keeps almost answering: what does it mean to belong to God — and what does that cost you? The Aquila Report runs a piece on Ephesians 1 this week, pressing hard on what divine possession actually implies. Not comfort. Claim. Tim Challies follows it with a meditation on mosaics — God's masterpiece made from broken pieces — which sounds devotional until you sit with it. The brokenness isn't incidental to the image. It's the material. Ligonier says fear never left the church; Christians just learned to dress it up. That's a harder word. If Challies is right that God works through fracture, Ligonier wants to know why we're still pretending we're whole. The Gospel Coalition enters through the AI debate but lands somewhere older — human dignity isn't threatened by the machines, because God already settled it. The real threat is that we forgot he did.

Mere Orthodoxy has two pieces this week that belong next to each other. One reads Psalm 11 as a rebuke to ministry built on utility alone — antifragile ministry that holds when the foundations are shaken. The other argues that rest can't be purchased, only received. Both are pushing against the same habit: the church reaching for tools when it needs posture. Relevant Magazine says something similar from the ground level — a piece on motherhood and exhaustion that won't let hope collapse into sentiment. That question hasn't gone away — earlier this week we were watching it surface through desire and formation, asking whether our minds are even capable of wanting the right things. World Magazine carries a quieter story: a Chinese pastor released, eight still imprisoned. One miracle closer, the headline says. Small phrase. It refuses to let the church forget what belonging to God sometimes looks like from the outside of a cell.
