Daily Pulse

July 22, 2026

Today the church is asking what salvation actually costs — and whether Christians have quietly lowered the price. Reformation21 goes to the thief on the cross and finds something bracing: a man with no time, no track record, and no religious resume who somehow got it exactly right. That's the whole point. Meanwhile The Aquila Report is asking whether people who've had decades do get it right — or whether familiarity with the cross has quietly become indifference to it. Relevant Magazine frames the same pressure in practical terms: your calendar is a theological document, and most of us won't like what it says about us. These pieces aren't in conversation with each other, but they're circling the same conviction — that real faith has a shape you can see, and a lot of what passes for it doesn't.

Rachael Denhollander is back in the conversation, and Relevant Magazine lets her say plainly what needs saying: the church's culture of forgiveness isn't just being misused by predators, it's being structurally weaponized. That's a harder claim than most outlets are willing to print. 9Marks takes a gentler but related angle — the Bible's pattern of failed fathers isn't incidental, it's the whole story of grace breaking through dysfunction. Neither piece is optimistic about human nature. Both are oddly hopeful about God. From Ukraine, a church leader in First Things warns that some conflicts defy negotiation — not every enemy is a dialogue partner, and pretending otherwise is its own kind of faithlessness. That question hasn't gone away: earlier this week we were watching it surface in how Christians think about discernment and pace. The pressure is the same. The stakes keep getting higher.
