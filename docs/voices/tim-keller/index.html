Tim Keller

Founding pastor of Redeemer Presbyterian Church, New York, 1989 to 2017 | Author of The Reason for God and The Prodigal God | Wrote on justice, the decline of evangelicalism, doubt and sexuality

About

Timothy Keller was ordained in the Presbyterian Church in America in 1975 and pastored West Hopewell Presbyterian Church from 1975 to 1984. He and Kathy Keller planted Redeemer Presbyterian Church in New York City in 1989, and he was its senior pastor until he stepped down in July 2017, after which he served full time with Redeemer City to City. He cofounded The Gospel Coalition and wrote 31 books, including The Reason for God, The Prodigal God and The Meaning of Marriage, written with Kathy Keller. He died on May 19, 2023, at age 72.

Source: In memoriam, timothykeller.com · About, Gospel in Life · Profile, The Gospel Coalition · Obituary, Christianity Today (May 19, 2023). Last updated November 13, 2026.

  1. A Biblical Critique of Secular Justice and Critical Theory
    Life in the Gospel · July 31, 2020

    Sets libertarian, liberal, utilitarian and postmodern critical theories of justice against a biblical account grounded in God's character and argues each secular theory rests on unsupported assumptions about human nature.

  2. The Decline and Renewal of the American Church: Part 2: The Decline of Evangelicalism
    Life in the Gospel · January 25, 2022

    Traces the shrinking of American evangelicalism to secularization, political polarization, abuse scandals, its record on race and separatist habits, and names grounds he sees for renewal.

  3. Reconstructing Faith: Christianity in a New World
    Life in the Gospel · October 14, 2022

    Argues that deconstructing an inherited faith can lead to a stronger reconstruction rather than unbelief, and urges doubters to scrutinize secular alternatives as closely as their own beliefs.

  4. The Bible and same sex relationships: A review article
    Redeemer Report · 2015

    Reviews books by Matthew Vines and Ken Wilson that argue Scripture permits same-sex relationships, answers their main arguments in turn and holds the traditional reading.
