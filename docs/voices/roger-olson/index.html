Roger Olson

Professor of Christian theology at Truett Seminary, Baylor, 1999 to 2021 | Author of The Story of Christian Theology | Writes on Arminian theology, evangelicalism and historical theology

About

Roger E. Olson taught Christian theology for 40 years at three Christian universities and retired in 2021 from Baylor University's George W. Truett Theological Seminary, where he was professor of Christian theology from 1999 and Foy Valentine Professor of Christian Theology and Ethics. Before Truett he taught for 15 years at Bethel University in Minnesota. He is a graduate of North American Baptist Seminary and earned a PhD at Rice University in 1984, with research under Wolfhart Pannenberg at the University of Munich. He is the author of more than twenty books, including The Story of Christian Theology and The Journey of Modern Theology, and writes the blog Roger E. Olson at Patheos.

Source: Good and Faithful Servants (retired faculty), Truett Seminary, Baylor University, 2024 · Endowed professorship announcement, Baylor University news, 2013. Last updated November 12, 2026.

Start here

  1. Don't Hate Me Because I'm Arminian
    Christianity Today · September 6, 1999

    Argues that Reformed theologians who deny that Arminians are authentically evangelical are fracturing the evangelical coalition, and calls both camps to mutual respect.

  2. Calvinism and Arminianism Compared
    Society of Evangelical Arminians (from his Patheos blog) · November 13, 2018

    A teaching handout for Sunday school and small groups that defines classical Calvinism and Arminianism side by side and argues that the real dividing line is not free will but the character of God.

  3. Arminianism is God-Centered Theology
    Society of Evangelical Arminians · November 29, 2010

    Argues that classical Arminianism affirms human depravity and total dependence on grace, and that it rejects Calvinist determinism chiefly because it would make God the author of sin.

  4. Arminianism FAQ 1 (Everything You Always Wanted to Know…)
    Society of Evangelical Arminians (from his Patheos blog) · July 8, 2014

    Explains classical Arminianism in question and answer form, covering prevenient grace, resistible grace, conditional election and where it sits between Calvinism and semi-Pelagianism.
