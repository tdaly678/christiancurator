Mark Dever

Senior pastor of Capitol Hill Baptist Church since 1994, founder of 9Marks | Author of Nine Marks of a Healthy Church | Writes on church health, membership and pastoral ministry

About

Mark Dever has served as senior pastor of Capitol Hill Baptist Church in Washington, DC, since 1994. He founded 9Marks, a ministry to churches and church leaders, in 1998 and served as its president; his church's staff page now lists him as president emeritus of 9Marks. He holds a PhD from Cambridge University and degrees from Gordon-Conwell Theological Seminary and The Southern Baptist Theological Seminary, which named him its inaugural Duke K. McCall Professor of Pastoral Leadership in 2019. His books include Nine Marks of a Healthy Church, The Gospel and Personal Evangelism, Discipling and The Compelling Community.

Source: Staff page, Capitol Hill Baptist Church · Author page, 9Marks · Author page, Crossway · Profile, The Gospel Coalition · News release, The Southern Baptist Theological Seminary (2019). Last updated November 12, 2026.

  1. If You’re Thinking about Leaving a Church . . .
    9Marks · September 30, 2014

    Advises members thinking of leaving a church to pray, talk with their pastor and test whether their reasons are personal or doctrinal, and if they go, to leave without dividing the congregation.

  2. Where’d All These Calvinists Come From?
    9Marks · June 18, 2014

    Traces the growth of Reformed theology among young American evangelicals to influences from Spurgeon and Lloyd-Jones to the Banner of Truth reprints, the inerrancy debates, Packer, Sproul, MacArthur and Piper.

  3. Twelve Questions to Ask Yourself Before Posting Something Online
    9Marks · August 29, 2016

    Sets out a checklist of questions about edification, tone, motive, audience and counsel to work through before publishing online.

  4. Neither a Republican Nor a Democratic Church
    9Marks · November 14, 2016

    A sermon introduction from the Sunday after the 2016 election that urges a congregation to love members who voted differently and not to tie the church to either party.
