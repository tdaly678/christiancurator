Michael Horton

Reformed Theologian
Quick Facts

Michael Scott Horton (born May 11, 1964) is the J. Gresham Machen Professor of Systematic Theology and Apologetics at Westminster Seminary California and host of the White Horse Inn radio program. An ordained minister in the United Reformed Churches in North America, he holds a PhD from the University of Coventry & Wycliffe Hall, Oxford, and is a leading voice in the Reformed confessional tradition in America. Horton is known for sharp critiques of American evangelicalism's drift into therapeutic, moralistic, and consumer-driven Christianity. His magnum opus The Christian Faith and his shorter Pilgrim Theology have become widely used systematic-theology textbooks. He has been a strong defender of covenant theology, the means of grace, and a confessional Reformed identity over against revivalism and pragmatism.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions about Michael Horton

What denomination is Michael Horton?

Horton is an ordained minister in the United Reformed Churches in North America (URCNA), a confessional Reformed denomination. He teaches at Westminster Seminary California, where he has been the J. Gresham Machen Professor of Systematic Theology and Apologetics since 1998.

Is Michael Horton a Calvinist?

Yes. Horton is a confessional Reformed theologian who subscribes to the historic Reformed confessions, including the Three Forms of Unity. He is one of the leading defenders of covenant theology and confessional Calvinism in America.

What is the White Horse Inn?

The White Horse Inn is a long-running radio show and podcast Horton founded that discusses Reformation theology and its application to the modern church. It is part of Sola Media, which also produces Modern Reformation magazine and Core Christianity.

What is Michael Horton known for?

Horton is known for his systematic theology textbook The Christian Faith, his critique of American Christianity's consumer-driven and therapeutic tendencies in Christless Christianity, and his advocacy of a churchly, confessional Reformed faith over against revivalism.
