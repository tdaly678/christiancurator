Best Christian Articles on Preaching

Why does expository preaching matter — and what distinguishes a sermon that forms people from one that merely informs them?

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

Preaching is the central act of gathered Christian worship. Not performance, not inspiration — preaching. The Reformers recovered this conviction over against a medieval church that had reduced the sermon to a minor liturgical element, and the evangelical tradition has maintained it: when God's Word is faithfully proclaimed, God himself is speaking to his people. That is an extraordinary claim, and it carries extraordinary implications for how sermons are prepared and delivered.

Expository preaching — preaching that takes the main point of a biblical passage as the main point of the sermon — is not simply one homiletical method among many. It is the method that most faithfully represents what Scripture is: the word of God to his people, with authority over every dimension of their lives. When a preacher replaces the text's agenda with his own, he usurps an authority that does not belong to him. When he submits his agenda to the text's, he stands under Scripture with his congregation and lets God speak.

The practical implications are wide-ranging. Expository preaching prevents the preacher from preaching only his favorite topics. It forces him through difficult passages he might otherwise avoid. It trains the congregation to read their Bibles with increasing confidence. And over time, it produces a people formed by the whole counsel of God rather than by a curated selection of the preacher's insights. The articles below make this case from Scripture, history, and pastoral experience.

1
Why Must We Preach Expositionally?
· 9Marks
The essential case for expository preaching as the normative evangelical method. This article argues that preaching with biblical authority is synonymous with true expository preaching — that when the preacher submits his message to the text's message, he is not merely using a technique but honoring the nature of Scripture itself as God's authoritative Word. The strongest single article for understanding why expository preaching is not optional.
2
A Biblical Case for Expositional Preaching
· 9Marks
Grounds the argument in Scripture itself. Where does the Bible show us what faithful preaching looks like? This article traces the pattern from Nehemiah 8 through Jesus's synagogue sermon in Luke 4 to Paul's charge to Timothy to 'preach the Word' — and shows that what unites all of these is the same basic posture: the preacher as herald of a message that is not his own.
3
Five Effects of Expository Preaching on a Church
· 9Marks
A congregational rather than a homiletical perspective. What happens to a church over years of faithful expository preaching? This article identifies five effects: difficult passages don't get avoided, the preacher can't ride his hobbyhorse, the congregation learns to read Scripture for themselves, the text sets the agenda rather than the preacher's preferences, and the whole counsel of God shapes the whole life of the congregation.
4
Expositional Imposters
· 9Marks
A bracing and necessary piece on the ways sermons can look expositional while actually not being so. Two common imposters: the topical sermon dressed up with Scripture citations (the text illustrates the preacher's point rather than generating it), and the running commentary that explains the passage without ever landing on a main point. A corrective for preachers and a vocabulary for congregations to evaluate the preaching they receive.
5
Evangelistic Expository Preaching
· 9Marks
Addresses a common objection: doesn't expository preaching only serve believers? This article makes the case that faithful biblical exposition is profoundly evangelistic — that through it, non-Christians have Satan's lies exposed, God's truth revealed, their own hearts searched, and Christ's grace magnified. The sermon that is most faithful to Scripture is also the sermon most likely to convert.
What the Conversation Adds Up To

These articles converge on a single conviction: preaching is not a performance in which a gifted communicator shares his insights about life and faith. It is a herald's act — the proclamation of a message received from another, with the authority of the one who sent it. That posture of submission to Scripture is what makes preaching different from everything else that happens in the pulpit, and it is what makes expositional preaching the method most faithful to what Scripture claims to be.

For congregations, the long-term effect of faithful expository preaching is formation in the whole counsel of God — not just the parts the preacher finds interesting or the congregation finds comfortable. That breadth is a gift. And for preachers, the discipline of submitting week after week to the text's agenda is not a constraint on their creativity; it is the school in which they learn to hear God speak.
