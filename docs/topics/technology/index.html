Best Christian Articles on Technology

Smartphones, social media, and AI are reshaping daily life — how does Christian wisdom speak to a digital world?

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

Every generation of Christians has faced new tools that reshape attention, community, and the conditions of human flourishing. What makes the smartphone era distinctive is the degree to which these devices are designed — by engineers with clear economic incentives — to maximize the time users spend inside them. The smartphone is not simply a tool; it is an environment that forms its inhabitants, often in directions that cut against the grain of Christian discipleship.

The evangelical conversation on technology has moved beyond early-adopter enthusiasm and reflexive rejection toward something more theologically serious. Drawing on thinkers like Neil Postman, Jacques Ellul, and Andy Crouch, the best evangelical writers on technology ask not just "what can we do with this?" but "what is this doing to us — developed further in our guide on technology and discipleship — a question developed further in our guide on technology and discipleship?" The question of formation — what kind of people our habits are making us — is fundamentally a discipleship question, and it belongs in the church.

The stakes are specifically spiritual: fragmented attention makes sustained Scripture reading harder; comparison-driven anxiety is structurally built into social media; parasocial online relationships increasingly displace embodied community. These are not productivity concerns. They are spiritual formation concerns. The articles below are the best evangelical reckoning with what faithful, intentional engagement with technology actually requires.

Key Questions This Topic Addresses
  • How is the smartphone reshaping Christian attention, desire, and community?
  • What is the difference between using technology and being formed by it?
  • What does a 'tech-wise' church look like — and why does it matter?
  • How should Christians think about AI in ministry and spiritual formation?
  • What practices help Christians engage technology intentionally rather than habitually?
The Evangelical Conversation, Curated
1
The Case for Tech-Wise Churches
Noah Senthil · The Gospel Coalition
The best recent article on what it means for a local church to cultivate wisdom about technology as a community. Senthil argues that digital technology is designed to be addictive and that churches must establish intentional boundaries — not to moralize, but to protect the embodied, face-to-face fellowship that cannot be replicated online. A practical and theologically grounded call to action for church leaders.
2
Smartphone Addiction and Our Spiritual ADD
· Desiring God
A bracing look at what fragmented digital attention does to our capacity for prayer, Scripture meditation, and sustained worship. As digital media splits attention into smaller units, this article argues that Christians must operate from a God-saturated worldview — developing countercultural discipline to prioritize embodied worship and deep formation over digital convenience.
3
7 Ways Smartphones Can Enhance Your Spiritual Life
· The Gospel Coalition
A necessary counterbalance to pure critique — a practical look at how smartphones can be used intentionally as tools for spiritual growth. Scripture apps, sermon podcasts, prayer reminders, accountability tools: these are real goods when used purposefully. The key framing: are you using the tool, or is the tool using you?
What the Conversation Adds Up To

The best evangelical thinking on technology converges on a distinction that should shape every Christian's engagement: the difference between using a tool and inhabiting a medium. A hammer can be picked up and put down; a smartphone is an environment you live inside, and environments form you whether you intend them to or not. The call is not technophobia but something more demanding — countercultural intentionality about how, when, and why we engage the digital world.

For the local church, this means the technology conversation belongs in discipleship relationships and from the pulpit, not just in parenting books. Churches that ignore the formative power of digital media are ceding significant territory in the discipleship of their members to engineers in Silicon Valley whose interests are not aligned with spiritual maturity.
