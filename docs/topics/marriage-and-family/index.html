Best Christian Articles on Marriage & Family

What does the Bible actually teach about marriage, family, and why the household still matters for the mission of God?

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

Marriage and family are among the most contested cultural battlegrounds of the present moment, which makes the church's need for theological clarity both more urgent and more politically fraught. The evangelical tradition has a rich account of marriage — grounded in Genesis 1–2, developed through the biblical narratives, and given its deepest interpretive key in Ephesians 5's account of marriage as an image of Christ's covenant love for the church. That account is not simply conservative social policy; it is a theology of what marriage is for and what it reveals.

The New Testament's most radical claim about marriage is that it exists to display the gospel. Paul's argument in Ephesians 5 is not primarily about gender roles or household order; it is about the relationship between Christ and the church, which marriage is meant to embody and proclaim. This means that how a Christian marriage is lived — with sacrificial love, faithful commitment, and mutual submission to Christ — is itself a form of gospel witness in the world.

For families, the stakes are equally high. Parents are the primary disciplers of their children — not the church, not the school, not the youth group. The home is where faith is first learned, first practiced, and first tested. The evangelical conversation on family has recovered this conviction in recent decades, pushing back against the outsourcing of children's formation to programs and professionals. The articles below represent the best of that recovery.

Key Questions This Topic Addresses
  • What is the biblical foundation for marriage, and what does Genesis 1–2 actually establish?
  • How does Ephesians 5 transform the evangelical understanding of marriage — and what does it require?
  • What is the theological significance of permanence, fidelity, and exclusivity in Christian marriage?
  • How should Christian parents think about their role in discipling their children?
  • What does the Bible say about family when marriage is broken or absent?
The Evangelical Conversation, Curated
1
A Biblical View of Marriage
· The Gospel Coalition
The authoritative reference essay on marriage from a biblical-theological perspective. This piece traces the scriptural account of marriage from creation through the fall, through Israel's covenant history, to the New Testament's fulfillment in Christ — and argues that marriage is a God-given institution whose meaning is primarily the portrayal of covenant love between Christ and his Church. Essential for understanding the evangelical theological account, not just the ethical positions.
2
The Story of Marriage in Seven Verses
· Desiring God
A beautiful biblical-theological meditation on seven key passages that trace the arc of marriage through Scripture. From the creation of woman in Genesis 2 to the marriage supper of the Lamb in Revelation 19, this piece shows that marriage is not a merely human institution but a divinely authored story with an eschatological destination. One of the more devotionally rich pieces in this collection.
3
Teach Them About Marriage Before the World Does
· Desiring God
A clarion call to parents and churches to be proactive rather than reactive in teaching children about marriage. The culture is not neutral on this question — it is forming children with a comprehensive alternative vision. This article argues that the church must give children a thick, positive, beautiful account of God's design for marriage before the world's account fills the vacuum.
4
How Does God Define Family?
· Desiring God
Addresses the definitional question directly: when the Bible talks about 'family,' what does it mean — and how does that biblical understanding challenge contemporary assumptions? The article also helpfully addresses how the Christian community functions as a family for those who are unmarried, widowed, or whose biological families are broken, showing that the household of God encompasses and exceeds every human household.
5
Marriage Is Meant for Making Children Disciples of Jesus
· Desiring God
A sermon-length treatment of the missional dimension of Christian marriage and parenting. Marriage and family are not private institutions for personal flourishing; they are the primary context in which the next generation of disciples is formed. This piece connects the Great Commission to the household — arguing that faithful parenting is one of the most significant missionary acts a Christian can undertake.
What the Conversation Adds Up To

What unites all of these articles is the conviction that marriage and family are not simply personal arrangements for human happiness — they are theological institutions with cosmic significance. Marriage displays the gospel. The family is a disciple-making community. The household is the primary context in which faith is transmitted across generations.

The practical implication is that churches cannot treat marriage and family as private matters outside their pastoral concern. How members of the congregation are husbands and wives, fathers and mothers, is not incidental to their discipleship — it is central to it. And the church's teaching on marriage must be positive and thick, not merely reactive to cultural pressure. The goal is not to defend a position but to commend a vision.
