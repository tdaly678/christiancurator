Best Christian Articles on Spiritual Warfare

The New Testament is saturated with spiritual conflict. But how Christians engage the demonic has become one of the most divisive questions in evangelical theology.

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

The New Testament presents a world at war. The ministry of Jesus is characterized by direct confrontation with demonic forces; Paul instructs the Ephesians to "put on the full armor of God" for battle against "the powers of this dark world and the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms." Peter warns that the devil "prowls around like a roaring lion." Whatever theology of spiritual warfare a Christian holds, the biblical data demands that it take the demonic seriously. The question is not whether the spiritual realm is real, but how Christians should engage it — and evangelical Christianity has produced strikingly different answers.

The major fault line runs between those who see spiritual warfare primarily as the ordinary Christian life of faith — resisting temptation, standing firm in the gospel, praying, and fleeing sin — and those who advocate direct confrontation with demonic forces through deliverance ministry, spiritual mapping, and intercession aimed at territorial spirits. The former view is associated with Reformed and cessationist evangelicalism; the latter with the charismatic movement and what C. Peter Wagner called the Third Wave. A mediating position, common in evangelical renewal movements, holds that the gifts of the Spirit including discernment and deliverance are still operative but must be exercised with careful biblical grounding and theological sobriety.

These differences are not merely academic. They shape how churches pray, how Christians understand temptation and mental health, and whether deliverance ministry has any place in evangelical church life. The articles here represent the best of evangelical engagement with the demonic — from careful exegesis of the relevant passages to honest engagement with the charismatic tradition and its excesses. Questions about how the Spirit works today are also central to our coverage of spiritual disciplines and prayer.

Key Questions This Topic Addresses
  • What does the New Testament actually teach about demonic activity and spiritual warfare?
  • Is "strategic level spiritual warfare" — warfare against territorial spirits — biblical?
  • What is the difference between ordinary spiritual conflict and demonic possession or oppression?
  • Should evangelical churches practice deliverance ministry — and if so, how?
  • How does the armor of God in Ephesians 6 define the Christian's primary weapons in spiritual conflict?

Spiritual Warfare

The reference essay for evangelical thinking on spiritual warfare — comprehensive, exegetically careful, and theologically balanced. This piece establishes the biblical framework: Christians are in a real war against real spiritual enemies, but the primary weapons are the ordinary means of grace — the Word, prayer, community, and the proclamation of the gospel. Essential for grounding the topic in Scripture before engaging more contested questions.

Spiritual Warfare for the Ordinary Christian
· Desiring God
A clarifying piece that locates spiritual warfare in the mundane rhythms of Christian life rather than in dramatic confrontation with demonic forces. The argument: Satan's primary strategy is not spectacular attack but quiet deception — and the Christian's primary defense is not dramatic deliverance but faithful, prayerful immersion in the truth. A necessary corrective to approaches that see warfare primarily as spiritual crisis intervention.
3
What the Bible Says About Territorial Spirits
· Crossway
A direct engagement with one of the most contested claims in charismatic spiritual warfare theology — that demonic forces are assigned to geographic regions and that Christians can dislodge them through targeted prayer. The piece engages the Daniel 10 passage that is most often cited in support of territorial spirits and argues for a more modest, Word-centered view of intercession.

Can Christians Be Demon-Possessed?
· The Gospel Coalition
One of the most practically important questions in evangelical spiritual warfare theology: can a genuine Christian be demonized or possessed? The piece surveys the biblical data carefully and distinguishes between demonic influence (which can affect believers) and possession (which the New Testament seems to rule out for those indwelt by the Spirit). Pastorally sensitive and exegetically responsible.

· 9Marks
A rare piece that locates spiritual warfare within the context of local church life rather than the individual Christian's private experience. The argument: the church gathered — preaching, sacraments, prayer, discipline — is itself the front line of spiritual warfare, the body through which Christ advances his kingdom against the gates of hell. A robustly ecclesiological account of the battle.
What the Conversation Adds Up To

Evangelical spiritual warfare theology is most helpful when it holds two truths in tension: the demonic realm is real and active, and the primary Christian response to it is not dramatic but ordinary. The armor of God in Ephesians 6 consists not of special spiritual techniques but of truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, the Word, and prayer — the normal equipment of the Christian life. A theology of spiritual warfare that requires special gifting, dramatic confrontation, or elaborate protocols has departed from the Pauline account.

This does not mean that deliverance has no place in pastoral ministry — the New Testament clearly shows Jesus and the apostles casting out demons — but that the charismatic tradition's elaboration of deliverance as a primary ministry strategy goes beyond what Scripture warrants. The war is real. The weapons are ordinary. The victory is already secured in Christ's death and resurrection — and our task is to stand firm in what has already been won, rather than to achieve new ground through spiritual technique.

The Evangelical Debate
Word-Centered Warfare vs. Confrontational Deliverance Ministry

The sharpest controversy in evangelical spiritual warfare theology concerns whether Christians should directly engage and confront demonic forces — through deliverance prayer, spiritual mapping, territorial intercession — or whether the biblical model of warfare is primarily indirect: standing firm in truth, prayer, and the gospel. This debate maps roughly onto the cessationism/continuationism divide but is not identical to it.

Position One
Word-Centered Spiritual Warfare
D.A. Carson · John MacArthur · R.C. Sproul · Reformed evangelicalism
Spiritual warfare is fundamentally about truth and its proclamation. Satan is defeated by the Word of God — preached, believed, and lived. Direct confrontation with demonic forces ("I bind you, Satan") is not the New Testament pattern for ordinary Christians; it is the province of apostolic authority now completed. The armor of God is truth, righteousness, and prayer — not specialized techniques. Deliverance ministries that promise dramatic demonic encounters often produce spiritual instability rather than health.

Position Two
Continuationist & Charismatic Spiritual Warfare
C. Peter Wagner · Francis Chan · Jack Deere · Charismatic evangelicalism
The New Testament pattern of Jesus and the apostles includes direct engagement with demonic forces — and the church has authority and responsibility to continue this ministry today. Gifts of discernment, deliverance, and healing are still operative. Strategic-level spiritual warfare acknowledges that demonic forces organize around geographic regions and ideological systems, and that targeted intercession can displace them. The lack of deliverance ministry in cessationist churches explains patterns of bondage and spiritual defeat.
