Pornography & Sexual Purity

Is pornography primarily a spiritual problem, a neurological addiction, or both — and what does genuine freedom actually look like?

Last updated: April 17, 2026

TL;DR

Most evangelicals view pornography as both a spiritual issue rooted in sin and a neurological habit that rewires the brain's reward pathways. Genuine freedom involves repentance, renewing the mind through Scripture, addressing underlying wounds, and cultivating healthy sexuality within God's design—not merely white-knuckling behavior modification.

Pornography & Sexual Purity is a significant topic in evangelical Christianity that touches on core convictions about faith, Scripture, and Christian practice. The evangelical conversation about Pornography & Sexual Purity involves genuine theological disagreement among faithful Christians who share a commitment to biblical authority and the gospel of Jesus Christ.

What makes the conversation about Pornography & Sexual Purity so important is its implications for how we live out our faith in the modern world. Different evangelical perspectives on this topic reflect different understandings of Scripture, tradition, and the application of biblical truth to contemporary challenges.

Understanding the evangelical debate over Pornography & Sexual Purity requires careful attention to the biblical text, engagement with church history and theology, and a willingness to learn from Christians who interpret Scripture differently. The goal is not mere agreement but a deeper grasp of what Scripture teaches and how to apply it faithfully.

The Evangelical Debate

Three Evangelical Perspectives on Pornography & Sexual Purity

Evangelical Christians affirm Scripture's authority, yet they interpret what it teaches about Pornography & Sexual Purity in different ways. Here are three significant evangelical approaches to this important topic.

Position 1
Spiritual Sin & Warfare
Albert Mohler · Ed Welch · John Piper · Kevin DeYoung · Tim Challies
This position emphasizes that pornography is fundamentally a spiritual problem—an act of idolatry and lust that enslaves the soul and violates God's design for sexuality and covenant faithfulness. Proponents stress the importance of repentance, spiritual warfare through Scripture and prayer, and heart transformation through grace rather than self-effort alone. They contend that addressing the neurological dimension apart from spiritual rebirth leaves the core problem unresolved.

Position 2
Neurological Addiction
Jay Stringer · Heath Lambert · Mark Shaw · Heath Adamson · Joe Rigney
This perspective views pornography addiction through the lens of neuroscience and psychology, emphasizing how repeated exposure rewires the brain's reward pathways, creating genuine physiological dependency. Proponents advocate integrating biblical truth with trauma-informed care and neurological understanding, arguing that sustainable freedom requires addressing underlying wounds and beliefs while understanding that the brain can be renewed through consistent practice and community support.

Position 3
Integrated Care (Spirit & Brain)
Ed Welch · Jay Stringer · Heath Lambert · Mark Laaser · Russell Willingham
This integrated position affirms that pornography involves both spiritual rebellion and neurological habituation, requiring simultaneous attention to heart transformation and brain healing. Proponents argue that genuine freedom comes through gospel repentance combined with understanding how the brain works, community accountability, trauma processing, and cultivation of healthy sexuality—recognizing that sanctification engages the whole person: spirit, mind, and body.

What the Conversation Adds Up To

These three approaches represent genuine evangelical engagement with Pornography & Sexual Purity. All three are committed to Scripture, to the gospel, and to faithfulness in the church. What distinguishes them is how they interpret and apply biblical truth to this particular question.

The conversation about Pornography & Sexual Purity ultimately reflects deeper convictions about Scripture, theology, and the Christian life. Engaging thoughtfully with different evangelical perspectives on this topic helps the church understand what Scripture teaches and how to live it out faithfully in our time.
