Best Christian Articles on Anxiety & Fear

Anxiety has become one of the defining experiences of our generation. What does Scripture offer — and where does the gospel meet the fearful soul?

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

Anxiety disorders are now among the most common mental health diagnoses in the developed world — and evangelical Christianity has not been immune. The question is not whether Christians struggle with anxiety but what resources the gospel brings to that struggle, and how the church should respond. The answers evangelicals give to these questions reveal deep commitments about the nature of the human person, the sufficiency of Scripture, and the relationship between faith and medicine. These questions are explored further in our coverage of mental health and faith.

At one end of the spectrum, biblical counseling advocates like Ed Welch and Paul David Tripp insist that anxiety is ultimately a heart issue — a form of disordered fear that reflects misplaced trust — and that the primary resources for addressing it are the gospel, Scripture, community, and prayer. This view does not deny that anxiety has biological components but insists that the Bible's categories are sufficient for understanding and addressing what is at root a spiritual problem. At the other end, integration advocates argue that anxiety disorders often have neurological and psychological dimensions that require professional therapeutic intervention, and that a church that dismisses medication and psychotherapy is leaving people to suffer unnecessarily.

These debates are not merely clinical — they are pastoral. How a church thinks about anxiety determines whether anxious members feel understood or shamed, supported or abandoned. The articles here represent the best evangelical thinking on anxiety — from the theological foundations to the pastoral wisdom that struggling Christians most need. The question of how the gospel speaks to fear connects to our broader coverage of suffering and providence and prayer.

Key Questions This Topic Addresses
  • Is anxiety a sin, a weakness, a medical condition — or some combination of all three?
  • What does Scripture offer the anxious person — and what are the limits of "just trust God" as pastoral counsel?
  • When should a Christian consider medication for anxiety — and what should the conversation with their church look like?
  • What is the biblical counseling approach to anxiety, and how does it differ from clinical psychology?
  • How can churches create environments where anxious people feel safe rather than shamed?
The Evangelical Conversation, Curated
1
Don't Waste Your Anxiety
· Desiring God
One of the most pastorally rich evangelical treatments of anxiety available. This piece argues that anxiety, like suffering, can be redeemed — that the fearful soul is positioned to discover resources of grace that the confident person never needs to find. Written with genuine theological depth and pastoral warmth, it takes anxiety seriously as a real experience without reducing it to mere unbelief.
2
What the Bible Says About Anxiety
· The Gospel Coalition
A careful biblical survey of what Scripture actually teaches about fear and anxiety — from the Psalms of lament to Paul's 'do not be anxious about anything' to Jesus' words about worry in the Sermon on the Mount. The piece is honest about the tension between the Bible's commands not to fear and the reality that many faithful Christians experience persistent anxiety, and it offers a framework for holding both truthfully.
3
Anxiety and the Sufficiency of Scripture
· CCEF
Ed Welch's characteristic approach: taking the sufferer seriously, engaging the medical and psychological literature honestly, and arguing that the Bible's categories — the deceitfulness of the heart, the reality of spiritual warfare, the promises of God's presence — provide the most adequate framework for understanding and addressing anxiety. Essential reading for understanding the biblical counseling approach to the topic.
4
Anxiety, Medication, and Faith
· The Gospel Coalition
A balanced evangelical treatment of the question of medication for anxiety — one that neither dismisses psychiatric medication as faithlessness nor treats it as a straightforward solution to what is fundamentally a spiritual problem. The piece argues for a both-and approach: medication can reduce physiological anxiety symptoms to a level where spiritual work becomes possible, but it cannot address the underlying disordered loves and misplaced fears that Scripture identifies as anxiety's root.
5
The Gospel for the Anxious Soul
· Crossway
A gospel-centered pastoral essay that locates anxiety's cure not in technique or therapy but in a deepened encounter with the character of God. The argument: the anxious person is ultimately afraid that the worst will happen and that God will not be enough if it does. The gospel's answer is not a guarantee that the worst won't happen but a guarantee that God will be sufficient when it does — and a record of his faithfulness to deliver on that promise.
What the Conversation Adds Up To

The evangelical conversation on anxiety has matured significantly in recent years. The cruder versions of the debate — 'just trust God' vs. 'get therapy, stop spiritualizing' — have given way to more nuanced engagement with both the spiritual and biological dimensions of anxiety disorders. The best evangelical thinking holds together what the Bible says about fear with what medicine and psychology have taught us about the brain — without collapsing either into the other.

What the gospel distinctively offers the anxious person is not a set of coping techniques but a changed relationship to the future. The anxious person is, at bottom, someone who is trying to manage an unmanageable future through their own resources. The gospel announces that the future belongs to God — and that the God to whom it belongs has demonstrated his trustworthiness in the cross. This does not make anxiety disappear, but it does provide a foundation from which the anxious person can engage their fear without being destroyed by it. The church's task is to embody that foundation: to be a community of people who take anxiety seriously and point, concretely and repeatedly, to the One who is sufficient for it.

The Evangelical Debate
Biblical Counseling vs. Psychology Integration: What Does the Anxious Christian Need?

The most practically consequential debate about evangelical approaches to anxiety concerns whether the church should primarily offer Scripture-based counsel or whether it should embrace clinical psychology and psychiatry as partners in care. This debate is a specific instance of the broader conflict between the biblical counseling movement and integrationist approaches to mental health — and it has real consequences for how churches pastor struggling members.

Position One
Biblical Counseling (Nouthetic Model)
Jay Adams · Ed Welch · Paul David Tripp · CCEF
The Bible is sufficient for understanding and addressing the problems of the human soul, including anxiety. While neurological factors are real, anxiety is ultimately a heart issue — disordered fear reflecting misplaced trust and disordered loves. The church's primary resource is the gospel applied to the specific lies anxiety tells. Professional psychology often addresses symptoms without addressing root spiritual causes, and its secular anthropology can undermine genuine healing.

Position Two
Psychology Integration
Mark McMinn · Diane Langberg · Christian psychologists & psychiatrists
God reveals truth through both Scripture and general revelation — including what neuroscience and psychology have discovered about how anxiety works. Anxiety disorders often have neurological components that medication can address, and cognitive-behavioral therapy has proven effectiveness even in Christian populations. A church that refuses to integrate these resources is withholding help from suffering people. Integration does not compromise Scripture — it honors the God who created the brain and redeems the whole person.
