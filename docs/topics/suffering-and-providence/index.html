Best Christian Articles on Suffering & Providence

Why does God allow suffering — and what does Christian faith have to say that the world doesn't?

Curated by Christian Curator · Updated regularly

Suffering is the great test of every theology. Abstract doctrines about God's sovereignty and goodness become very concrete when a diagnosis arrives, a marriage fractures, or a child dies. The question "why does God allow this?" has been asked in every culture and century — but the Christian answer is distinctive, because it locates its answer not in a philosophical argument but in a crucified man.

The doctrine of providence — God's active governance of all things, including the darkest — is not simply comfort theology. It carries sharp edges. It means that no suffering is random, that nothing has slipped outside God's awareness, and that the God who ordains suffering is the same God who entered it. For evangelical Christians, this is not a problem to be explained away but a mystery to be inhabited: "For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses" (Heb. 4:15).

The evangelical conversation on suffering spans the pastoral and the doctrinal. Some articles ask the hard philosophical questions about why a good God permits evil. Others focus on the lived experience: how does a church community walk faithfully with someone who is suffering? How does a pastor prepare a congregation before suffering arrives? What is the difference between the comfort the prosperity gospel offers and the comfort Scripture actually gives? The articles below represent the best of that conversation, from writers who have engaged suffering both theologically and personally.

The Evangelical Conversation, Curated
1
Don't Underestimate the Doctrine of Providence
Stephen Witmer · The Gospel Coalition
The best single-article case for why the doctrine of providence matters in everyday life. Witmer argues that believing God governs every event — not just the big ones — is not a cold abstraction but a source of genuine pastoral comfort and moral transformation. For Christians who have heard the word "sovereignty" but never felt its weight, this is the piece to start with.
2
Why Suffering Pushes Us Toward or Pulls Us Away from God
Trevin Wax · The Gospel Coalition
An honest reckoning with why the same experience of suffering causes one person to cry out to God and another to abandon faith altogether. Wax explores the spiritual dynamics at work in suffering — and what it means to orient ourselves toward God in pain rather than away from him. Particularly useful for pastors and counselors walking with people in crisis.
3
The Old Testament Is a Story of Providence
Kevin DeYoung · The Gospel Coalition
A biblical-theological piece that traces the thread of divine providence through Israel's entire history. DeYoung's gift is making complex theology concrete, and here he shows how every reversal and deliverance in the Old Testament narrative testifies to a God who governs without controlling in the manipulative sense — whose sovereignty is expressed through and not around human agency and history.
4
Comforting Lies About Suffering: How the Prosperity Gospel Hurts People
Desiring God
A pointed critique of the health-and-wealth gospel's approach to suffering — and why its comfort is a cruelty. The prosperity gospel promises that faith protects against suffering; when suffering comes anyway, believers are left with either spiritual crisis (God failed me) or self-condemnation (I failed God). This article names that harm and returns to the cross as the only ground for genuine hope.
5
Five Ways Pastors Can Prepare Their Churches for Suffering
9Marks
Suffering is not a pastoral emergency to manage but a reality to prepare for. This practical piece addresses what pastors can do before suffering arrives — shaping congregational culture, preaching a full-orbed theology of suffering, and building the relationships that enable genuine care. Essential reading for church leaders who want to shepherd well when it's hardest.
6
Enter into the Trials of Fellow Church Members
9Marks
Drawing on 1 Corinthians 12:26 ("if one member suffers, all suffer together"), this article makes the case that Christian suffering is inherently communal — and that the imagination is a spiritual capacity that enables us to enter into pain we are not currently experiencing. A compelling vision of what embodied church membership looks like when someone in the congregation is suffering.
What the Conversation Adds Up To

What separates the Christian account of suffering from every secular alternative is not a better answer to the problem of evil, but a different category of answer altogether: the cross. A God who stands outside suffering and explains it is a philosopher's God. The God of Scripture is a God who entered suffering in the person of Jesus Christ — which means suffering is not evidence that God is absent, but potentially evidence that God is unusually present.

Practically, these articles converge on a vision of the local church as the primary context for suffering well. The doctrine of providence is not an individual consolation prize; it is a communal resource, sustained through the preached Word, the Lord's Supper, and the patient presence of brothers and sisters who refuse to leave.
