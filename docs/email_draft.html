The Daily Pulse

Today the church is staring at a credibility gap: Christians are suffering for the Gospel overseas while Christian institutions at home keep failing the people they're supposed to serve. The Christian Post profiles a former Muslim who lost his family and his freedom rather than stop sharing Jesus. The Gospel Coalition is running prayers for the persecuted this week, names and places most American evangelicals will never recognize. That faithfulness is real and documented. So is this: The Roys Report is covering a 15-year sentence handed to an evangelical financier who defrauded investors, and an Ohio megachurch pastor fired for sexual misconduct. Two stories, same wound. The witness the church holds up abroad keeps getting undercut by what's happening inside the building.

Desiring God has a piece on the tyranny of preferences, the slow way consumer instincts colonize the soul. It's a formation argument, and it fits. A New Mexico megachurch publicly endorsing Republican candidates is testing the Johnson Amendment right now, and The Guardian is asking why Lance Wallnau, one of Trump's loudest evangelical backers, has gone quiet ahead of the midterms. These are not separate problems. They're the same pattern: the church reaching for political leverage it doesn't quite have, while its own house is in disorder. Earlier this week we were watching desire and discernment surface as the central question. Today it shows up with harder edges. What the church wants, what it endorses, who it covers for, and who it prays for: those answers don't all point the same direction right now.
