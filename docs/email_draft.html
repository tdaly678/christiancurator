The pro-life movement is having a reckoning, and the church doesn't agree on what it means. Abortion measures won in Missouri, Nevada, Virginia, and Idaho last night, and First Things is calling the results a disaster, arguing the movement has lost its cultural footing and needs to rethink everything. Christianity Today is more measured, tracking the ballot results state by state without the same alarm. Conservative pastors, per World Magazine, are processing what a blue wave means for their public witness, some worried, some insisting the church was never supposed to win elections. The disagreement is real: First Things thinks political strategy failed, while some pastors seem to wonder whether political strategy was ever the right frame.

Russell Brand's rape conviction in London is landing hard in evangelical spaces because Brand was baptized and publicly claiming faith when the assaults occurred, per The Roys Report. Roys also broke the story of four women accusing a North Point Community Church musician of sexual abuse. Two stories, one pattern: celebrity and institutional proximity to Christian identity providing cover while harm accumulates. On March 26 we were watching the church wrestle with whether sentimentalism was replacing genuine faith. These cases put a sharper edge on that question. Proclaimed belief that outruns actual accountability is not discernment failure, it is a structural one, and the church keeps having to learn it again.
