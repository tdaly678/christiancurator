The Daily Pulse

Today the church is reckoning with institutional failure — who holds power, who gets hurt, and whether Christian organizations are actually safe. The Roys Report broke the news that former Acts 29 CEO Stephen Timmis and his wife Janet now face eight rape charges in the UK. Same outlet, same week: a whistleblower says Compassion International has no functioning process to remove sponsors accused of grooming vulnerable children. Two stories. One pattern. The institutions Christians built to do good have, in these cases, become places where the vulnerable have no exit. That's not a communications problem. It's a structural one — and it raises the question the church has been circling for years without fully answering: what accountability actually looks like when the accused hold the authority.

A New Mexico megachurch is now openly endorsing Republican candidates, testing the Johnson Amendment's federal ban — which puts institutional power of a different kind on the table. Christian blogger Samuel Sey, arrested by ICE and removed to Canada, is a reminder that how the church relates to state power cuts differently depending on who's being processed. And this Sunday, the global church is being asked to pray for 388 million Christians facing high-level persecution — many of them under governments their Western brothers and sisters are busy trying to influence. Modern Reformation ran a piece this week on God's left hand — Luther's way of talking about how God governs the world through civil structures, distinct from how he governs the church. It's an old framework. It doesn't resolve any of this. But it clarifies what's actually being confused.
