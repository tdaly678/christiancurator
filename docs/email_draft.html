The Daily Pulse

Today the church is asking whether it actually shows up for the people it claims to love, or whether it protects itself instead. The Roys Report published two pieces in close succession that make that question unavoidable. A whistleblower says Compassion International ignored years of internal warnings about child photos being weaponized for AI sextortion. A Mexican woman is suing a Missouri mission that ran abusive children's homes for decades. These are not isolated stories. They are a pattern: institutions built to protect the vulnerable, caught protecting themselves. The question isn't whether abuse happens. The question is what a ministry does when it hears the first warning.

Desiring God is asking whether missionaries cost their children too much, which is a different version of the same problem: who bears the weight of the institution's calling, and who gets to decide. That question runs alongside a harder one Lifeway Research surfaced this week. Two in three churchgoers failed a basic Bible literacy quiz. If the people sitting in the pews can't name the sources of what they believe, they can't evaluate what they're being asked to trust. That gap between institution and formation has been in this feed for weeks now. On March 24 the conversation was about suffering and whether the church believes God works through it. It still is. The difference today is that some of the suffering is coming from inside the institution doing the claiming.
