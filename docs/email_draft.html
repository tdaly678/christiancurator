The Daily Pulse

Evangelical writers are pressing this week on what faithful witness looks like when Christianity is losing its cultural majority, and the answers pull in different directions. New Pew Research projections, covered by Christianity Today, show Christians falling below majority status in both the U.S. and Canada by 2070, with Islam projected to become the world's largest religion globally by 2035. The numbers are real, but the more interesting argument is about what they require of the church. Lifeway Research's Catherine Renfro frames evangelism success around faithfulness rather than fruitfulness: the evangelist's job is obedience, not results. That reframing matters precisely because the demographic slide makes results harder to claim.

Lana Silk at The Christian Post takes the argument further by looking at Iran, where following Jesus carries legal and social cost, and finding there a church that is neither anxious nor passive about its minority status. The contrast with Western comfort is the point. 9Marks contributor Calvin Anthony argues that the gathered church, formed week by week around Scripture, is what sustains a people who can no longer count on cultural reinforcement. Thomas West's review at The Gospel Coalition adds an apologetic note: the shrinking of Christian cultural presence may sharpen rather than weaken the intellectual case, since Christianity still explains what other frameworks cannot. That question, whether a minority church witnesses more clearly than a majority one, was alive in our March 23 dispatch on Gen Z and belonging, and the Pew numbers give it new weight.
