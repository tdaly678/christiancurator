The Daily Pulse

Evangelical writers are pressing this week on what it actually means to be secure in salvation, and the conversation reaches from systematic theology down to Tuesday-night struggles in the pew. Cornelis Venema at Ligonier Ministries grounds assurance in divine preservation: God's work in Christ includes God's commitment to keep those he saves, which means assurance rests on his fidelity, not ours. Chalmers' Blog answers the question most people actually carry into that argument, asking whether someone who keeps falling into the same sin can still be a Christian, and the answer is yes, though it is not a cheap yes. The two pieces are not in conflict, but they are answering different fears. Venema speaks to the person wondering whether God will hold on; Chalmers speaks to the person wondering whether the evidence of their life disqualifies them from being held.

Kelly King at Lifeway Research brings the same question into congregational practice, arguing that women's ministry fails when it treats transformation as a byproduct of a well-run event rather than its purpose. That concern tracks closely with what earlier this week we were watching several writers argue about sentimentalism and genuine faith: good feelings and busy calendars are not the same as formation. Cassie Achermann at The Gospel Coalition adds a pastoral note, arguing that assurance does not require resolved circumstances, only the presence of Immanuel through unresolved ones. Chris Gehrz's Sunday devotion on Matthew 5 and 6 sits quietly at the edge of this, meditating on the unforgiven person who cannot receive what is being offered. What the week's writing holds together is a conviction that security in Christ is both a doctrine to be taught and a reality to be practiced toward specific people in specific trouble.
